Five form submissions are about to be written into a contact sheet. Each one fails in a different way, and none of the failures raises an error. Build the scenario one decision at a time and watch the sheet after every run.

Every write starts with a read

Until now your scenarios have moved data forward: a trigger fires, something happens, something gets sent. The moment a scenario has to update something that already exists, it has to find it first — and the finding is where almost everything goes wrong. Make's documentation is clear about the mechanics; what it cannot tell you is what your data actually looks like.

What people assume

A search returns the record

One lookup, one row, one update. This is true of your test data, because you test with records you know are there.

What a search returns

Zero rows, one row, or many

All three are normal, none of them is an error, and your scenario has to have an answer for each. Most scenarios have an answer for exactly one.

Nothing in this lab errors. Every run you are about to do finishes with a clean history.

Build it, one decision at a time

Each step adds one setting and runs all five records against the sheet. Settings you have not reached yet stay at their naive default, which is the point — you are watching a scenario become correct, not starting from a correct one.

Run anything

All five settings, unlocked. Change one at a time and watch which record breaks — that is the fastest way to learn what each setting is actually protecting you from.
