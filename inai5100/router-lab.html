The Router Lab

Until now your scenarios ran one path. A router lets the flow make decisions — and introduces two failure modes that are invisible until they cost you. Build the routes, run a real queue, and find out what happened to the tickets nobody handled.

What a router actually does

A router takes one incoming record and offers it to every branch you have defined. Each branch has a filter. This is the part people get wrong:

Rule 1

Every branch is checked, independently

A router is not an if/else chain. It does not stop at the first branch that matches. If a record passes three filters, it goes down all three — and the work happens three times.

Rule 2

A record matching nothing disappears

No branch passes, no error, no log entry. The run completes successfully and the record is simply gone. This is the most expensive bug in this course because nothing tells you it happened.

Your job below is to make both of those happen on purpose, so you recognize them when they happen by accident.

What this teaches

Three habits that carry into Mini-Project 4 and into anything you build at work.

Habit 1

Always add a fallback route

A catch-all branch that logs or notifies rather than acting. It costs one module and converts a silent disappearance into something you can see.

Habit 2

Check your filters for overlap

Two branches that can both be true for one record will both run. Sometimes that is what you want. Usually it means a customer gets two emails.

Habit 3

Assume fields will be blank

A filter on a field that is sometimes empty fails for exactly those records. Blank data is the usual cause of a silent drop — not a broken rule.

The question to carry

Who notices?

If a record vanishes tonight, how long until a person finds out — and who is that person? That question is where Week 7 starts.
