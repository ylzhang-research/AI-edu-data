The Handoff Audit

Three working automations are being handed to you. Nothing is broken, nothing has errored, and in each case somebody has told you it is fine. You get one conversation with the person leaving. Decide what you need to know before you say yes.

Inheriting is not the same as building

When you build something, you know why every filter is there. When you inherit it, the filters are just facts on a screen with no reasons attached — and the reasons left with the person who wrote them. Week 7 ended on the sentence that an automation with no owner eventually stops working. This is the other half of that: an automation with a new owner who was never told anything also stops working, just more slowly and more confusingly.

What a handoff usually is

“It works, don't touch it”

A reassurance, a login, and a scenario you have never opened. Everything that made it work is in somebody's memory, and that somebody is leaving on Friday.

What a handoff should be

A short list of answered questions

Who owns the credentials. What the undocumented filter is for. Who controls the inputs. Who finds out when it fails. Five answers, written down, and the thing is genuinely yours.

Your attention in that conversation is the scarce resource. Asking nine questions is not better than asking the right five.

Read the dossierEverything visible to you in Make, and nothing else. Read it before you look at the list.
Build your requisitionTick the things you need answered before you accept ownership. Fewer, sharper beats more.
Submit and compareYou will find out which of your questions were load-bearing, which were merely useful, and which spent your one conversation on nothing.
