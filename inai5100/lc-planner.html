Plan the circle

A plan that describes only the good version is a proposal. This tool walks both halves — the circle itself, and the part where somebody objects. Fill it in, download it, and write your plan from it.

Part A · The circle

Start with the room. Everything else is easier once you know whose Tuesday you are changing.

Who and what

A real one. Your employer, a nonprofit you volunteer with, a business you own, a student group, a church.
A role, not a department. This is the person your plan is written for.
Four to eight, by role, with a reason each is there. Mixed roles — a circle of only IT people produces IT answers to business problems.
One named role that owns scheduling and follow-up. This is the ownership piece.

Cadence and session

Something you would defend to someone protecting their calendar.
The shape of the time. Who talks, what gets shown, what gets decided. “Discuss AI tools” is not a format.
A specific place, and who outside the circle would ever find it there.
Something you could observe in a few months — and that could come back negative. This is the question most plans dodge.

Part B · The hard part

Twenty of the hundred points live here, and it is the half most plans leave out. A plan that anticipates resistance is something someone can act on.

Obstacles and cost

A specific, likely one. Calendar pressure, a manager who sees it as unbilled time, a prior initiative that fizzled and left people cynical.
A named role, not “management” — and give them a legitimate reason, not a strawman.
Hours per person per month, including the convener's preparation time. Approximate is fine; vague is not.
Shows you know which part is the core and which parts are decoration.
What may and may not go into AI tools, given your organization's actual obligations.
